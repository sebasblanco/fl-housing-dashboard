The core relationship

Two mechanics explain almost every price cycle, including 2008–09. Everything in the tiles below is tagged by which one it feeds.

↕Inverse to price

When this indicator rises, prices tend to fall — and vice versa. These are the supply-and-cost-side levers.

  • Mortgage rates ↑ → monthly payment ↑ → buyer pool shrinks → prices soften
  • Months of supply ↑ → sellers compete for fewer buyers → prices soften
  • Days on market ↑ → buyer leverage ↑ → prices soften
  • Insurance / HOA cost ↑ → effective price ↑ → demand shrinks at the same list price
⇄Proportional to price

When this indicator rises, prices tend to rise with it — these are demand-side confirmations.

  • Sales volume / pending contracts ↑ → prices firm up or accelerate
  • Net in-migration ↑ → more buyers chasing the same stock → prices firm
  • Wage growth ↑ → affordability improves → market can sustain higher prices
  • New construction permits ↑ → future supply ↑ → cools *future* price growth (lagged inverse)

Reading the whole board

Two things changed materially since Nov 8, and they point opposite ways. First, the rate relief ended: the 30-yr fixed fell a fourth straight week to 7.11% on Nov 12, then snapped back 17bp to 7.28% on Nov 19 — the highest since July, and up a full point from 6.26% a year ago. The 10-yr Treasury eased that same week, so this was lender spread rather than the bond market, which makes it the kind of move that can unwind as easily as it appeared. Second, and more important, demand broke: October existing-home sales came in at 3.75M SAAR, down 5.5% on the month and 8.8% YoY — the slowest pace since August 2010 — while Redfin’s pending sales are down 15.3% YoY, the weakest in its records since 2021. National months of supply rose to 5.1 not because listings piled up but because the sales denominator collapsed; Florida active single-family listings are actually down 7.4% YoY. That distinction matters for anyone reading this as a 2008 setup — 2008 was an inventory glut forced out by resetting ARMs, and this is a buyer strike with locked-in owners sitting still. It is why price has barely moved: the national median still printed +0.4% to $416,700 and Florida single-family rose 2.2% YoY to $420,000, even as volume fell off a cliff.

Where price has broken is exactly where the cost shock is: condos. Statewide condo/townhouse prices are down 3.3% YoY to a $290,000 median against 8.0 months of supply, and county-level readings are far worse — Miami-Dade at 12.7 months, Broward near 12.9. The SB 4-D / SIRS compliance deadline is now about five weeks out (Dec 31, 2026), and the forced-listing wave this dashboard has been watching for has still not shown up in the data. Fort Lauderdale remains the clearest single turning-point signal on the board at −5.8% YoY on Redfin’s four-week metro window — and that figure is now reconciled against the city-level sold data rather than left hanging (see the note under the chart). Florida is still cutting prices at roughly twice the national rate (~42–46% of listings vs. 20.7% nationally). The honest read for a buyer waiting on a dip: the dip is real but it is concentrated in condos and in a handful of metros, the single-family side is stalling rather than falling, and the rate reversal just removed the one tailwind that was starting to help.
