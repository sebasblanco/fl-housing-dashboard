The core relationship

Two mechanics explain almost every price cycle, including 2008–09. Everything in the tiles below is tagged by which one it feeds.

↕Inverse to price

When this indicator rises, prices tend to fall — and vice versa. These are the supply-and-cost-side levers.

  • Mortgage rates ↑ → monthly payment ↑ → buyer pool shrinks → prices soften
  • Months of supply ↑ → sellers compete for fewer buyers → prices soften
  • Days on market ↑ → buyer leverage ↑ → prices soften
  • Insurance / HOA cost ↑ → effective price ↑ → demand shrinks at the same list price
⇄Proportional to price

When this indicator rises, prices tend to rise with it — these are demand-side confirmations.

  • Sales volume / pending contracts ↑ → prices firm up or accelerate
  • Net in-migration ↑ → more buyers chasing the same stock → prices firm
  • Wage growth ↑ → affordability improves → market can sustain higher prices
  • New construction permits ↑ → future supply ↑ → cools *future* price growth (lagged inverse)

Reading the whole board

The headline change this week is a real, if modest, rate pullback — three straight weekly declines bring the 30-yr fixed to 7.18%, down from the 7.32% late-October peak — while every supply-side metric (days on market, price cuts, months of supply) keeps reading more buyer-favorable, not less: Florida single-family supply moved to ~5.2 months and Redfin now counts 63% more sellers than buyers statewide, the widest gap in its records since 2013. The biggest reversal in the city data: Fort Lauderdale, which this dashboard flagged just a week ago as having pulled ahead of Miami on +14.1% YoY growth, is now named by Redfin's own November report as the single largest YoY price decliner of any U.S. metro (−5.9%) — about as clean a "turning point" signal as this dashboard is built to catch, though the exact new price level hasn't been reconciled yet (see the asterisk below the chart). Florida condos remain the clearest 2008-style distress signal on the board, and that story got worse, not better, this week — supply widened back out to ~9.7 months statewide (Miami-Dade alone near 14.1 months), with the SB 4-D special-assessment compliance deadline (Dec 31, 2026) now under two months out. No subprime/ARM-reset wave, no collapse in sales volume, and current owners are still largely locked into low rates rather than forced sellers — the 2008 comparison remains a condo-insurance story, not a credit one.
